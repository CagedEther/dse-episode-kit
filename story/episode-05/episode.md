# Episode 05 — The Crew
Working draft · Target length: 110 seconds

## What this episode is for
To show that complex tasks require breaking work into specialized sub-agents, while revealing the bottleneck that occurs when human review cannot keep pace with the pipeline.

## Structure
| Beat | Timing | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening hook | 0:00–0:18 | The single assistant fails the deadline; she shuts the draft. |
| Setup | 0:18–0:38 | The spy crew analogy: dividing complex work among specialists. |
| Development | 0:38–1:15 | Introducing the four specialists down the line to build the site. |
| Turn | 1:15–1:35 | The Evaluator flags a conflict; the handler steps in to make the call. |
| Cliffhanger | 1:35–1:50 | The machine accelerates until she becomes the bottleneck. |

## Voice-over
[00:00] The client's site is due by morning. Her single assistant tried to do everything at once: map the structure, write every word, choose the style, and critique itself. The result was scattered, weak, and flat. She closed the draft.

[00:18] Every spy drama has the quiet moment where the handler spreads files across the table. Nobody sends one person on a five-part heist. You bring in the sniper who tracks the entire field. The one who cracks the safe. The one who walks past the guard. When work grows complex, you break it apart and assign each piece to a specialist.

[00:38] She gathers four sub-agents.

[00:43] The Architect maps the territory. Every room, every route, and the single path a visitor must take.

[00:51] The Copywriter fills the rooms. She stays strictly inside the lines the Architect drew.

[00:59] The Designer directs the eye, balancing weight and space.

[01:07] And the Evaluator builds nothing. She arrives as a total stranger, testing every seam for weakness.

[01:15] By midnight, the system delivers. But the Evaluator flags the home page. The design is striking, yet the essential path is buried. The crew stalls in deadlock. Only the handler can decide. She restores the path and sends it back.

[01:35] The machine roars alive. The specialists take the rhythm and accelerate. Within an hour, thirty pages queue up on her desk. The crew is fast. She is slow. The handler who saved the mission is now the one stopping it.

## The crew

Drawn people, like the agents in episode 01. None of them wear her spiral earring, teardrop pendant, or black vest. One mark each. No names on screen; names go into the crew-reveal bubbles in post.

| Agent | Job | How you recognize her | Plate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Architect | Maps the rooms and the one path a visitor must follow. | Fine grid lines across one sleeve. | `visual-bible/characters/architect/front.png` |
| The Copywriter | Fills the rooms with every line the visitor reads, inside the map. | Ink-black fingertips on the writing hand. | `visual-bible/characters/copywriter/front.png` |
| The Designer | Gives the page its balance and decides where the eye lands. | Collar split half black, half white. | `visual-bible/characters/designer/front.png` |
| The Evaluator | Builds nothing. Walks the page as a stranger and reports what fails. | One eye always in shadow under a narrow brim. | `visual-bible/characters/evaluator/front.png` |

The single overloaded assistant in the hook is the pool character `man-cowlick`.

**The page.** One web page is drawn front-on in a thin browser outline, with the parts of a real site: logo block, menu dashes, a picture of an ink hill, a heading bar, ruled lines for paragraphs, three cards, and one solid black rounded-rectangle button, the one thing the client needs a visitor to press. Words are never letters. The same framing is used in SH03, SH12, and SH15: out of place, then clean with the button lost in a corner, then clean with the button where the eye lands.

## What the voice-over must not do
The voice-over must not describe hair, clothes, or props. It carries the plot while visual staging presents the figures.
